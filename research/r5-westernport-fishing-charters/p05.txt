Victoria Business for Sale Market Insights
Market insights updated for the September 2026 quarter. 
There are currently 3,412 businesses for sale in Victoria on Bsale, including cafes, restaurants, franchises, service businesses and established regional operations. New listings are added regularly, with more than 1,000 listings updated across Bsale every week.
Victoria is one of the largest business for sale markets on Bsale, with around one in four listings nationally. In the September 2026 quarter it also recorded more businesses marked as sold than any other state, accounting for one in three nationally.
Buyer demand strengthened too, with enquiries for Victorian businesses rising 35% year-on-year. Melbourne was one of the five Australian areas receiving the highest number of buyer enquiries on Bsale.
What Is the Average Asking Price for a Business in Victoria?
The average asking price for businesses for sale in Victoria was $680,506 in the September 2026 quarter, according to Bsale's market data, slightly above New South Wales ($673,143).
Regional Victoria sits well above the state average. Geelong & South West Victoria averaged $1.16 million, Bendigo & Goldfields $1.06 million, Gippsland $1.05 million and Shepparton & Hume $1.02 million, each across more than 100 listings.
Melbourne averaged $610,978, and the Mornington Peninsula was among the more accessible markets nationally, giving buyers a wide range of price points across the state.
Asking prices are advertiser-supplied, are not independently verified and may include stock, plant and equipment or property. 
How Is the Victoria Business Sales Market Performing?
Businesses marked as sold in Victoria rose around 35% year-on-year in the September 2026 quarter, and the state accounted for one in three businesses marked as sold nationally, the highest share of any state.
Melbourne led the increase, with businesses marked as sold up around 60%, reflecting strong turnover among cafes, restaurants and smaller service businesses. The Mornington Peninsula also recorded an increase of around 22%.
Buyer activity also rose, with the number of individual buyers making enquiries up around 38% year-on-year. Nationally, the median time between a business being listed and marked as sold on Bsale was 202 days .
Sold figures reflect listings marked as sold on Bsale, not independently verified transactions or final sale prices. Not all businesses are marked as sold when removed from Bsale. 
What Types of Businesses Are Buyers Looking for in Victoria?
Hospitality remains the largest category on Bsale, with approximately 28% of buyer enquiries nationally, and cafes led all categories for businesses marked as sold in the September 2026 quarter. Cafes for sale in Victoria sit alongside restaurants , retail and franchises .
National demand is also shifting towards service and trade businesses. Service business enquiries rose 62% year-on-year and automotive businesses 51%. These sectors include cleaning businesses and mechanics .
Businesses that run with less owner involvement are also in demand. Listings advertised as under management received around 30% more enquiries than the national average, while remote and home-based businesses attracted more than twice the average.
Industry enquiry trends are based on national Bsale activity and may differ within Victoria. 
Where Are Buyers Looking for Businesses in Victoria?
Melbourne attracted most of Victoria's buyer enquiries in the September 2026 quarter, with interest in suburbs such as Eltham , Bentleigh East , Preston and Berwick .
In regional Victoria, buyers enquired about businesses in Geelong , Wodonga , Shepparton and Ballarat & Grampians .
Buyers interested in an established business model can also view franchises for sale in Victoria .
Buying a Business in Victoria
Bsale has connected buyers with businesses advertised for sale across Australia since 2000, including Melbourne and regional Victoria.
Before buying a business in Victoria, review its financial performance, lease conditions, employee arrangements and any industry-specific licensing requirements. In regional markets, check what the asking price includes, as plant, machinery, vehicles or freehold property can lift the price considerably.
Buyers are increasingly requesting financial statements, information memorandums and confidentiality agreements earlier in the enquiry process. Having finance organised and professional advisers available can help buyers assess an opportunity and move through due diligence.

How do I get alerts for new businesses for sale?

What should I check before buying a business? 
Before you commit, check the financials (at least three years of profit and loss statements and BAS), the legal position (the commercial lease, licences and any council orders) and the operations (staff, systems and equipment).
Ask for the information memorandum early, have an accountant confirm the owner's earnings against the bank statements, and have a commercial solicitor review the contract before you sign.

Why is Victoria a great place to buy a business? 
Victoria is one of Australia's strongest and most balanced economies, making it an attractive place for business buyers. With over 6.8 million residents, the state provides a large and diverse customer base, driven by both metropolitan Melbourne and fast-growing regional centres such as Geelong, Ballarat, Bendigo and Shepparton.
The state has a highly diversified economy, with major activity across hospitality, retail, construction, transport, trades, health, technology, education and professional services. This spread of industries helps reduce risk for buyers, as the state is not dependent on one sector to drive growth.
Victoria has also experienced consistent population growth over the past decade, supporting demand for essential services, lifestyle businesses, and local operators. Strong regional migration continues to boost trading conditions outside the capital city, creating opportunities for buyers looking beyond Melbourne.
Infrastructure investment, a skilled workforce, well-connected transport networks and a steady flow of domestic and international visitors further strengthen the state's commercial environment. With its large population, diverse industries and stable economic foundations, Victoria offers strong conditions for anyone looking to buy and grow a business.

What businesses in Victoria are buyers most interested in? 
Buyer demand in Victoria is strong across a wide range of industries, driven by the state's large population, diverse economy and a mix of metropolitan and regional growth hubs. Hospitality remains one of the most active sectors, with cafes, restaurants, bakeries and specialty food operators attracting steady interest in Melbourne and regional centres. Retail, beauty, wellness, health and fitness businesses also perform well, particularly in high-traffic suburbs and lifestyle-driven regional towns.
Victoria's ongoing construction activity and population growth are fuelling demand for trades and service-based businesses, including building, maintenance, electrical, plumbing, cleaning, and home services. Professional services, childcare, health clinics and allied health practices continue to attract buyers due to predictable, year-round demand.
The state's strong creative, tech and education sectors also support growing interest in online, e-commerce and digital businesses, especially those offering flexibility and scalability. Regional areas such as Geelong, Bendigo, Ballarat and the Surf Coast are seeing increased buyer activity, with many seeking lifestyle-friendly businesses supported by expanding local economies.
Overall, buyers in Victoria gravitate toward established businesses with reliable cashflow, strong local customer bases and industries supported by long-term population and infrastructure growth.
Which areas of Victoria are best for owning a small business? 
Victoria offers a strong mix of metropolitan, regional and lifestyle-focused locations that are well suited to small business ownership. Melbourne remains the state's economic centre, with high demand across hospitality, retail, professional services, health, beauty and trades. Inner-city suburbs such as Fitzroy, Carlton, Richmond, South Yarra and St Kilda attract steady foot traffic and strong local spending, making them ideal for customer-facing businesses.
Beyond Melbourne, several regional hubs are emerging as powerful small-business locations thanks to population growth, infrastructure investment and more affordable operating costs. Geelong is one of Australia's fastest-growing regional cities, offering a thriving hospitality, retail and services sector. Ballarat and Bendigo both have expanding populations and strong local economies supported by education, healthcare, trades and tourism, making them attractive for owners seeking stability and lower entry costs.
Further north, Shepparton, Wangaratta and Wodonga offer opportunities in logistics, agriculture-linked services, retail and trades. On the coast, areas such as Torquay, Ocean Grove, Mornington Peninsula and Phillip Island attract lifestyle buyers interested in tourism, accommodation, hospitality and boutique retail.
Across Victoria, buyers tend to focus on areas with:
&bull; Strong population growth
&bull; Steady tourism or foot traffic
&bull; Affordable rents and operating costs
&bull; Expanding local industries and employment
These factors help create reliable customer bases and stable long-term trading conditions, making Victoria one of Australia's most opportunity-rich states for small business owners.
What Government support is available in Victoria? 
The Victorian Government offers a comprehensive range of grants, advisory services and business programs to support people looking to buy, operate or grow a business. Whether you're exploring finance options, improving digital capability, seeking mentoring or navigating regulatory requirements, these programs can make the transition into business ownership smoother and more informed.
Victoria also provides dedicated guidance for people buying an existing business, including licensing, legal steps, leasing considerations and compliance obligations. For ongoing support, business owners can access dispute resolution, commercial leasing help and general advisory services through the Victorian Small Business Commission.
