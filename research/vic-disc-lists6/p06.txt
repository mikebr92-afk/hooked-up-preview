Time on the seas 'a wonderful way of life' for long-time fisherman
By Tim Auld 
Updated February 17 2026 - 2:13pm , first published 1:59pm 
By Tim Auld 
Updated February 17 2026 - 2:13pm , first published 1:59pm

Life-long fisherman Dan Hoey loves sharing his joy for the water via his fishing charter business. Picture supplied
Dan Hoey is passionate about fishing, so much so, he started his own business which shares his joy with others. He goes Under the Auld Pump with TIM AULD.

AT A GLANCE
Born: December 15, 1977, in Port Fairy.
Wife: Nat. Children: Jasmin, Ashby, Charlie and Jack.
Parents: Cliff and Rosemary. Siblings: Julie, Christine and Phil.
Education: St Patrick's Primary School Port Fairy before going to Warrnambool's Brauer College. 
Sporting highlight: I love watching programs about fishing in amazing places around the world.
Dan, I note you're not far off reaching 50 years of age. Are you going to have a big party to acknowledge that achievement? 
I'm looking at organising a few things to celebrate the occasion but they're only at the early stages of planning at this stage.
What were your early years like growing up in Port Fairy? 
They were pretty good. I was like most of the lads in Port Fairy. I played a bit of junior footy with my mates. It's best to say I was an ordinary footballer.
I loved playing squash in the local competitions down at Southcombe Park and in the building at the back of Brookes Hardware. 
Squash was a big sport back in Port Fairy in my younger years. There were lots of young people playing the game. 
The other sport most of the young people took up was surfing. There would always be young people out surfing at The Passage or out the back of the lighthouse. 
Another sport I played in my younger years was basketball in the local competitions but I decided to concentrate on my working career.
Dan Hoey and wife Nat with Kevin, the salty dog. Picture supplied
What did you do for work? 
I left Warrnambool's Brauer College at the end of year 10 to be an apprentice carpenter for my uncle Artie Hoey.
It was the best thing I could do, being a carpenter. I was fortunate to work on some big commercial projects in Warrnambool and Port Fairy. 
We also built various residential houses around the district. We were never short of work. 
I reckon I worked with Artie for more than 14 years on projects, including Proudfoots in Warrnambool and down at the Lady Bay. They were big jobs especially doing the build down at Lady Bay after the pub was demolished. 
It was a massive job working down there. One thing I can always remember was the amazing views out to the ocean and back to Warrnambool you got as we were building the complex down at the Lady Bay.
Big projects we worked on in Port Fairy included the hospital, surf club and the Moyne Shire offices. They were all great jobs to work on as they offered so much versatility to give you experience in the building industry. 
The other thing I've done all my life is fishing. I've still got fond memories of fishing in Port Fairy's Moyne River as a youngster with my dad Cliff. 
We used to go up and down the river catching mullet in a small tinny before getting a bigger boat.
Little did I know back then that in 2013, I would start my own charter fishing operation. While working on my own charter fishing business, I also worked on the abalone boats for Rob Torelli from 2010 to 2018 in the south-west zone from Warrnambool to the South Australian border and that was a great learning experience.
Dan, you mentioned that you started your boating charter business in 2013. I suppose you've just come off the back of a few busy months with tourists wanting to fish the waters in this area? 
To be honest with you, we had a few down days over the holiday period as the weather wasn't the best for going out fishing. 
We're in a weather-dependent business and with bad south-easterly winds we couldn't take the boat out. The good days were good but there were a few bad ones. 
We're the only boat charter business in Port Fairy and Warrnambool, so that keeps us pretty busy. I think there are a couple of boat-charter businesses in Portland.
One of the funny things that often happens is passengers often think that we control what fish are out there each day but sadly that doesn't happen.
I've always wondered, do you have many people who get seasick? 
I would say we might get one person on every second or third trip suffering with seasickness but on a lot of occasions they finish up all right. 
I often think it's a mental problem - by that I mean people get in their mind before going out that they will get seasick, and they do. We usually have about eight passengers going out on the boat. 
There was one day we just got out the top of the Moyne River and a bloke was complaining he was crook so we just headed back into shore. The south-easterly winds usually cause the water to get really choppy. 
We get a lot of people, including international tourists, who want to come and fish in these waters. They're usually chasing gummy sharks or bluefin. 
April through to July are busy months for us as people want to catch the big tuna but the weather can turn pretty ordinary in July. We've had some passengers who have caught massive tuna. The strange thing is there are some times when the tuna are not in really deep water.
Dan Hoey with one of his catches. Picture supplied
Dan, do you often see whales while out on the high seas? 
We often see whales - all types, namely humpback, blue whales and killer whales. 
We might only see the killer whales two to three times a year but it's pretty special when we see them. 
There are various times when we see the whales getting big bait balls of fish - thousands of fish and the next minute the whale take one gulp and all the fish are gone. 
The southern right whales are also out there at different times of the year. I suppose one of my highlights occurred about 10 years ago. 
We did a video of some white-pointer sharks about eight kilometres offshore. The video attracted 650,000 views which was really special.
Dan, apart from fish, are there many other things out on the high seas? 
There's a lot of birdlife out there, amazingly there are lot of different types of birds including albatrosses. 
We also see a lot of dolphins. I just consider myself so lucky to be working outside in the open air, in nature watching some incredible sights. It's a wonderful way of life.
