Sydney Harbour Fishing Charter
Pickup Locations
We can collect your group from any public wharf around Sydney Harbour, so the day starts close to wherever you are. These seven are the wharves our charters use most often, with parking, transport and travel times for each.
Start close to home We pick up from any public wharf on Sydney Harbour
There is no single departure point for a Wahoo charter. Sydney Harbour has dozens of public wharves and jetties, and we can arrange to collect your group from almost any of them and drop you back at the end of the day. Whether you are in the eastern suburbs, the lower north shore, the inner west or the city, there is a wharf within a few minutes of you.
The seven wharves on this page are simply the ones we are asked for most, so we know them well: where to park, which ferry stops there, how long the run out to the fishing grounds takes and where to grab a coffee beforehand. If your group would rather board somewhere else, just tell us the suburb when you enquire and we will sort it out.
Pickup and drop-off around Sydney Harbour is included in the charter price, which is charged per boat rather than per person. We will confirm the exact wharf and meeting time when you book.
Tell us where you are 
Popular pickup points Our seven most-used Sydney wharves
Tap a wharf for parking, public transport, nearby cafes, accessibility and how long the run out to the fishing grounds takes from there.
Inner west
Thames Street Wharf, Balmain
A quiet working wharf on the Balmain peninsula with its own small car park, five minutes from the Darling Street cafes. The easiest pickup if you are coming from the inner west or across the ANZAC Bridge.
City centre
Aquarium Wharf, Darling Harbour
The CBD pickup, right beside SEA LIFE Sydney Aquarium and a short walk from Town Hall. Step-free ramp access, plenty of parking stations and the most scenic run out through the harbour.
Eastern suburbs
Double Bay Wharf
Rebuilt with step-free access and sitting right beside Steyne Park, a flat five-minute walk from the Bay Street and Knox Street cafes. Quick access to the Heads without the Watsons Bay parking crush.
Eastern suburbs
Watsons Bay Wharf
The closest wharf to the Heads, tucked in behind South Head in one of Australia's oldest fishing villages. Board here and you are in open water within minutes, which means maximum time with a line in.
Eastern suburbs
Rose Bay Wharf
The best parking of the eastern wharves thanks to the Lyne Park car park, with waterfront cafes either side and the seaplane base next door. A short, sheltered run down to the Heads.
Lower north shore
Mosman Wharf
A deep, sheltered bay at the bottom of Avenue Road with free untimed street parking nearby and a very short walk from the street to the pontoon. The pick for Mosman, Cremorne, Balmoral and the northern beaches.
Lower north shore
Neutral Bay Wharf
The Hayes Street wharf at the foot of Kurraba Point, about ten minutes by ferry from Circular Quay and the fastest wharf to reach if you are coming south over the Harbour Bridge.

Choosing your wharf Which Sydney wharf should your group use?
Two things decide it: how easy the wharf is for your crew to reach, and how much of your day you want to spend travelling to the fish.
Start with where your crew is coming from
If everyone is driving in from the north, Mosman Wharf and Neutral Bay Wharf save you the Harbour Bridge and the city traffic. Coming from the eastern suburbs, Rose Bay, Double Bay and Watsons Bay are all within a few minutes of each other, with Rose Bay the easiest to park at. From the inner west, Parramatta Road or the ANZAC Bridge, Thames Street Wharf at Balmain is the shortest drive. And if your group is scattered across the city, staying in hotels, or arriving by train, Aquarium Wharf at Darling Harbour is the one place everybody can find.
It is worth thinking about the whole group here, not just the driver. A wharf on a ferry route means anyone who wants a drink on board can leave the car at home, and the ferry ride in is a nice way to start the day.
Then think about the fishing you want to do
The further west you board, the longer the run out. That matters most for game fishing and deep sea fishing , where the grounds are well offshore and every minute counts. If you are chasing marlin or dropping deep, boarding at Watsons Bay or Rose Bay puts you through the Heads fastest.
For kingfish , sport fishing and jigging , much of the action is inside Sydney Harbour itself, on the reefs, moorings and washes, so a city or north shore wharf costs you almost nothing. And on a bucks party or corporate charter , where the day is as much about the harbour as the fish, boarding at Darling Harbour and cruising out past the Opera House and Harbour Bridge is part of the fun.
On the day How pickup and drop-off works
Simple and flexible. You choose the wharf, we do the rest.
• Tell us your preferred wharf, or just the suburb you are coming from, when you enquire 
• We confirm the exact wharf, the boat and your meeting time when you book 
• Arrive 15 minutes before departure so we can get you aboard and away on time 
• Half-day charters run 6:30am to 1:00pm or 1:00pm to 6:00pm, full-day game fishing 7:00am to 5:00pm 
• Rods, reels, bait and tackle are already on board, so travel light 
• BYO food and drinks are welcome, and gourmet catering is available on request 
• We drop you back at the same wharf unless you ask us to finish somewhere else 
• Pickup and drop-off around the harbour is included, our prices are per boat and not per person 
Good to know Pickup location questions
The things groups ask us most about where a Sydney fishing charter starts and finishes.
Where do Wahoo fishing charters pick up from in Sydney? 
We can pick you up from any public wharf around Sydney Harbour. The seven we use most often are Thames Street Wharf at Balmain, Aquarium Wharf at Darling Harbour, Double Bay Wharf, Watsons Bay Wharf, Rose Bay Wharf, Mosman Wharf and Neutral Bay Wharf. Tell us the suburb your group is coming from when you enquire and we will suggest the easiest one.
Can you pick us up from a wharf that is not on your list? 
Yes. The seven wharves listed here are simply our most popular pickup points, not the only ones. We can collect your group from almost any public wharf or jetty on Sydney Harbour, subject to the wharf being usable by our boat on the day and to tide, weather and ferry traffic. Just ask when you enquire and we will confirm it for you.
Does pickup from a different wharf cost extra? 
Our charter prices are per boat, not per person, and cover pickup and drop-off around Sydney Harbour. Some wharves are further from the fishing grounds than others, so if a pickup point adds a lot of travel time we will tell you how it affects your fishing time before you book. There are no surprises on the day.
Which Sydney wharf is closest to the offshore fishing grounds? 
Watsons Bay Wharf is the closest, sitting just inside South Head, so you are through the Heads and into open water within minutes. Rose Bay and Double Bay are next closest. Darling Harbour and Balmain are the furthest west, which means a longer run out, though it is a very scenic one past the Opera House and Harbour Bridge.
What time do we need to be at the wharf? 
Please be at the wharf 15 minutes before your departure time. Half-day charters run 6:30am to 1:00pm or 1:00pm to 6:00pm, and full-day game fishing runs 7:00am to 5:00pm. We confirm the exact wharf and meeting time when you book, and the skipper will be waiting to help you aboard.
Do you drop us back at the same wharf? 
Yes, we return to the wharf you boarded at unless you ask us to drop you somewhere else on the harbour. If your group is splitting up afterwards, or half of you want to finish near a pub, let us know and we will do our best to make the drop-off convenient.
Is there parking at the wharves? 
It varies a lot. Rose Bay has the roomiest car park of the eastern wharves, Mosman has free untimed street parking on the flat by the water, and Balmain has a small car park at the wharf itself. Watsons Bay and Neutral Bay are tight, and Darling Harbour is all paid parking stations. Each wharf page below has the detail, but signage and time limits change, so always check on the day. If parking looks difficult, the ferry is often the easier option.
Are the wharves wheelchair accessible? 
Most of the public wharves on Sydney Harbour have been upgraded with step-free gangways, including Aquarium Wharf, Double Bay, Rose Bay, Mosman and Neutral Bay. Thames Street Wharf at Balmain does not have step-free access. Getting from the wharf onto a fishing boat is a separate matter and depends on the tide and the boat, so please talk to us before you book if anyone in your group has mobility needs and we will work out the best wharf and boat for you.
