Size and possession limits in tidal waters
Download the Qld Fishing 2.0 app for the latest fishing rules.
'Tidal waters' are waters subject to tidal influence, such as those with:
• marine plants (mangroves, seagrasses, marine algae, or salt marsh)
• marina fauna
• salt or brackish water, or
• tidal ebb and flow (regardless of salinity).
On this page
General possession limit
If a tidal species is not listed on this page, a general possession limit of 20 applies.
How size and possession limits work
In Queensland, size and possession limits are in place to protect the long-term sustainability of our fisheries. Find out more about responsible fishing .
• There are minimum size limits and some maximum size limits for certain freshwater species.
• Make sure you know how to measure your catch correctly.
A possession limit is the total number of fish you can legally take and keep at any single time:
• It is not a daily limit.
• It includes fish you have at home, including those in your freezer.
Queensland also has closed seasons and waters in place.
Catch and release
Return undersized, oversized, no-take, or closed-season species to the water immediately to give them the best chance of survival. Watch our top tips for catch-and-release .
Fishing gear
To prevent overfishing, restrictions apply to the gear you can use. Make sure you're using the correct fishing gear .
Identify your catch
Not sure what species you've caught?
• Download the Qld Fishing 2.0 app 
• Use our fish species identification guide 
Your catch counts
Record your catch today to keep Queensland waters thriving for tomorrow.
Coral reef fin fish
There is a combined possession limit of 20 in total for all coral reef fin fish.
See the complete list of all coral reef fin fish species .
Skin removal and filleting rules:
• You can have whole (dead), gilled, gutted, or filleted fish on board a boat. Fillets must be at least 40cm long with skin and scales left on.
• You can only have a live fish on board a boat if it's going to be displayed in an aquarium (except coral trout and redthroat emperor).
• You cannot have fillets of blue spotted coral trout (Chinese footballer) on board a boat.
There are 2 five-day closed seasons in October and November each year.

Sharks and rays
Fin and tail removal and filleting rules:
• You can remove the fins and tails of some sharks and rays, but the rest of the body must also be on board the boat.
• You can divide a shark or ray into portions as long as an inspector can easily count the number of sharks or rays in your possession.
