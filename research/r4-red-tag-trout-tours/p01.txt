The ongoing adventures of a passionate wild trout fly fishing guide based in Tasmania,
Australia's best trout fishing destination.
Ray Curran guides for his one-man guiding operation Red Tag Trout Tours which caters to flyfishers from all over the world, who share a common goal: getting a wild brown trout to hand.

Season ending 
Well the backend of the season has been up and down a bit but overall quite rewarding for some.
Six beginners took on our March ‘Learn to Fly Fish’ Workshop at Herne, Lachlan, Michael, Trey & Corey, and Bettina and Elisa. By the end of the day they had all picked up the basics of casting and using wet and dry flies very well. From all accounts they will keep going with their new found love of the sport which is fantastic.
Daniel and his daughter Sally (from Boston, USA), enjoyed themselves very much at 28 Gates. Sally was a brand new beginner and picked up casting very quickly. There’s some big fish in there.
Peter and Elizabeth were back for their second trip this season which proved successful again over two days at Currawong.
Phil tried his hand at Herne later in March but the fish were hard to find sadly that day. Still, the experience and the day were worth it and Phil says he will be back.
Greg and Chris had better luck the next day at Currawong Lakes with some lovely fish taken, and missed.
It was lovely to see Lyn return to her passion for fly fishing and although the river we were on proved challenging the reward for Lyn, as she said, was simply to be back fly fishing (with a bonus of seeing two baby freshwater lobsters amongst the rocks).
Looking forward to the end of season guiding coming up then get everything set again for next season.
Speaking of which, I will be holding an information session at 6 pm on Thursday the 14th May at Hurley’s Fly Fishing Store, 489 South Road, Bentleigh VIC if anyone is in the area and would like to come along. Contact Gavin or Lachlan and let them know.

Tassie Challenge 
On the Board 
Long-standing clients of Red Tag Trout Tours are Peter and Elizabeth, who come out twice a year and enjoy challenging themselves and each other on Tassie’s rivers and lakes.
Day 1 
Day 1 saw them fishing at one of their favourite spots, Currawong Lakes, and this time the prize went to Elizabeth, who caught a couple of lovely fish, including a Rainbow and a Brown. Although the wind was strong and many more fish were seen, we were able to find those sheltered spots where Peter almost had one or two, but it wasn’t to be his day. 
Searching 
Still, just being on the water and seeing some beautiful fish was what they loved to do. The end of the day saw us having a lovely dinner at the Campbell Town Hotel, where Vanessa, Lachlan and Kat looked after us very well, thank you.
… I say chaps … 
Day 2 saw us head inland and on to a beautiful section of a river they had not fished before. Blue sky, lovely and warm day, insects all around, we walked a short distance before Elizabeth and Peter took turns to fish in this water. 
Winning grin 
Elizabeth almost had one straight up as she prepared her line and dropped the fly onto the water when wham, a fish came up and took it before she was ready…that one got away. But it wasn’t long before Elizabeth was on and landed a lovely brown on her Royal Wulff. 
Must be one here 
Then it was Peter’s turn, pulling out a lovely brown of about 1lb from under the shadows of the tree on the riverbank. That good old grasshopper did the trick. A few minutes later, under the same tree, Peter hauled in a magnificent brown of about 2 1/2lb. 
The End (for now) 
Beautiful, crystal clear water, a good level and lovely weather added to the day and by the end, Peter had two more, and we had a couple missed. All in all, a good day.
I know they will be back for more adventures in the second half of the season.

Fine fishing 
Recently, I had the pleasure of fishing with Roy and Gail, (seen above) long-time anglers with Red Tag. Over three and a half days, they fished Currawong Lakes and the Macquarie River with some success, despite blustery winds and a few memorable tales of fish that got away.
On the first day, a very windy day commenced. However, like most locations on Currawong Lakes, there is always a sheltered area where the weather is more favourable. This proved to be the case. Lake Currawong was challenging and yielded no results, so they moved to sheltered parts of Long Marsh and Lake Macquarie. While they saw some good fish, casting was difficult due to the strong winds. Some particularly large fish were seen near the shoreline but just outside their casting range.
At the end of the day, they returned to the Lake House for pre-dinner drinks and dinner. It was a relaxing evening with the prospect of more fishing the following day. On the second day, Roy and Gail set out early for a trip to the Macquarie River. The wind had subsided and the flow was strong. Upon reaching our location, we prepared and walked to the large pool. After observing no rises, we donned our wetsuits and commenced searching. Walking and casting all the way back to the car yielded no results, prompting lunch.
Here is Blog No. 1 for now. Photos: Currawong 8 – Gail stalking, Currawong 9 – Ray, Gail, Roy at Currawong Lakes, Currawong 18 – Roy & Gail outside the Lake Leake Hotel, Macquarie 5 – Roy with his nice Macquarie Brown and of course the car full and the bull.
Ray, Gail, Roy at Currawong Lakes 
We sat in the shade of a large willow tree, entertained by two falcons chasing off a large wedge-tailed eagle. The eagle ignored the falcons and perched in a large gum tree until they departed. At the same time, we observed two beautiful sea eagles soaring on the thermals above us.
During lunch, the loud sound of buzzing drew our attention to my wagon (the tailgate was open). A swarm of bees had flown into my car. A swift application of Aerogard to the car eliminated the bees.
After lunch, we returned to the river and resumed our fishing. Gail hooked into a small brown trout that was very active until released. Further downstream, she almost hooked another trout but was unsuccessful.
Roy then took over the fishing. After missing several trout, he spotted a fast-moving gutter and cast. He hooked into a 1½-pound brown trout, which he released.
WELL DONE ROY 
As evening approached, we returned to the Lake House to change for dinner. This time, we enjoyed a delicious meal at the Lake Leake Hotel.
On Day 3, we returned to the lakes at Currawong. Initially slightly breezy, the wind intensified throughout the day, and rain fell. The morning session was uneventful, but after lunch in a sheltered area, a large brown trout was hooked. It leapt to catch a flying insect before approaching Gail’s black spinner and taking it with a powerful strike.
Unfortunately, the tippet broke, and the trout escaped. Roy had one swim across his foot. As the rain approached late afternoon, we concluded the day with a barbecue. At the same time, the wind subsided, midges began to hatch, and fish were abundant. We watched them for approximately 15 minutes.
Celebrating their 55th wedding anniversary at the Lake Leake Pub 
Day 3 was particularly special as it marked the 55th wedding anniversary of Roy and Gail. A fine bottle of Shiraz was enjoyed by all that evening.
On Day 4, Roy and Gail had their final morning session. They caught a few fish, but none were within reach. With commitments later that day, they were taken to Launceston Airport to collect their rental car.
As Roy remarked, “Some days are successful, while others are challenging.”
Their previous visit a few years ago was a memorable day on the Rainbows. We eagerly anticipate seeing them again soon.

A Guiding Life 
Hi Everyone, the ‘Red Tag Story’ up until July 2023 as seen through the eyes of the founder, Roger Butler, prior to my acquisition.
The following is the Introductory Page and Front Cover of Roger’s Memoirs covering the first 27 years of Red Tag Trout Tours fly guiding service.
Introduction 
After almost three decades of running Red Tag Trout Tours then selling the business to Ray Curran back in July 2023, (enforced) retirement through peripheral neuropathy, it meant no more 6a.m. starts, with sun, wind, rain, occasional snow and gear packing – gee I miss it!
It also meant I had a head full of wonderful memories and a hard drive with thousands of images calling out for exposure.
These memoirs are not aimed at what or how to fly fish or guide, but do hold a few nuggets of information and tips that hopefully may encourage and / or give you confidence in stalking and presenting to those wily and wild trout of Tasmania.
All stories contained in this missive have at least a skeleton of truth onto which you can add the flesh of your own imagination.
Those featured here when contacted were enthusiastic in their support no one more so than …
“Dear Roger,
“I would be as happy as a seagull with a French fry to appear in your book. Someone should know about our rough and rowdy ways down by the riverside. At least one paragraph could be dedicated to your cooking skills.”
“Wishing you all the best with your ventures and raising a glass to your good health.” 
Love, Billy Connolly xxx
Tuesday, 28 September 2021

Rivers run through it 
Red Tag Trout Tours has been featured in The Australian in an article by Anabel Dean
Somebody is out there waiting for your hatching nymph,” says Ray Curran, eyes riveted on the tiny pink tuft drifting on a millpond. We’re standing waist-deep in waders in Tasmania’s Macquarie River on the kind of drowsy, insect-droning day that would induce a stupor were it not for this man’s unshakeable optimism.
Curran is an experienced fly-casting instructor so there’s no doubting his tale about wild brown trout that erupt from the water, curve into the air to hang at the top of an arc for a breathtaking second, then descend as mercurial shadows in the depths. I cannot imagine how a lure less than a centimetre long, mimicking the larval stage of insects, could excite such madness, but myriad tiny bugs drift in the warm air, and Curran is pointing urgently under a bended willow.

Good weather & fine fish 
I had Guy over two days in April, so we ventured to two very different spots on two different rivers. Both proved to be very successful for a range of reasons, with both fish to hand and the lovely places we went to.
On Day 1, the weather presented perfectly, and the flow was really good. From the outset, the fish were on, and within a short space of time, Guy had landed a beautiful brown. 
From then on, it was a similar pattern…cast, strike…sometimes landing them and sometimes not, but there was plenty of activity for that day, so by the time we finished, Guy had a lovely number to hand before we called it a day.
Day 2 we ventured to another great piece of water on a different river, different property, this time hoping for more great action to carry on from Day 1 and from the moment we kitted up it was on. 
Dropping his fly gently onto the river as he stripped line ready to cast, Guy inadvertently hooked up immediately only to lose the fish before he could settle. Not sure who was startled the most. 
With wide-open expanses, great flow and some beautiful runs, Guy sought out the spots that would most likely produce and by the end of the day he had a lovely number to hand as well as a tree protruding from the river bank but we won’t mention that one. 
As the sun went down and we walked back to the car in the fading light, Guy was certainly very happy with his two days and says he will be back.
