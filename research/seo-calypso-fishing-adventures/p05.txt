Giant Black Marlin Fishing in Cairns 
Giant Black Marlin Fishing In Cairns – Aboard Calypso
Cairns is recognised worldwide as the home of giant black marlin — the only place on earth where fish exceeding 1,000lb are reliably caught year-after-year. Each season, anglers travel from across Australia and around the globe for a chance to experience the heavy‑tackle marlin fishing on the Great Barrier Reef at its best.
Aboard Calypso , you’ll be fishing these legendary waters with an experienced and professional crew with a deep understanding of the fishery.
Giant Black Marlin Season – September to December
From September through December, the reef systems off Cairns plays host to an aggregation of black marlin along a section of the Great Barrier Reef called the Ribbon Reefs which runs north from Cairns up to the famous Lizard Island.
The unique combination of reef structure, bait aggregation, water temperature, and seasonal currents create conditions found nowhere else on the planet.
This is the reason the fish return to the reef year-after-year and is why Cairns remains the number one destination for giant black marlin fishing. 
Live aboard or mothership
Calypso can accommodate up to four guests live aboard .
For those seeking an elevated experience, mothershipping can also be arranged — offering additional space, comfort, and luxury while Calypso remains dedicated to fishing operations on the reef.
This option is ideal for extended stays, non‑fishing partners, or guests wanting a premium offshore experience without compromise.

Frequently Asked Questions 
When is the best time to fish for the giant black marlin 
The peak giant black marlin season in Cairns runs from September to December, with October and November traditionally the peak of peak season offering the highest chances of grander‑class fish.
Multi‑day charters are strongly recommended during peak season to maximise opportunities on the reef. 
How many days of fishing do you recommend? 
For anglers travelling to Cairns specifically to target giant black marlin, 3 to 5 days is considered a minimum timeframe. This allows for weather variability, fish movement, and the natural rhythms of the fishery — without rushing the experience.
It also gives us the flexibility to move with the fish, stay on the bite, and focus on the areas producing the best action at the time. Quite simply, the more days you fish, the greater the opportunity for a once-in-a-lifetime fish.
