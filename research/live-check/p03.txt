Fishing on the Mary River
The Mary River wetlands east of Darwin are some of the Territory's most famous barramundi water. Charters fish from the Shady Camp ramp — where a barrage separates the fresh and salt water — down towards the river mouth, as well as billabongs like Corroboree, part of the Mary system and about 90 minutes from Darwin.
As well as barramundi, anglers catch threadfin salmon, jewfish and, in the billabongs, saratoga. NT tourism notes that barra up to 1.4m are landed in the wetlands each year. The system also holds one of Australia's largest concentrations of saltwater crocodiles, which is one reason most visitors fish it with an experienced local guide.
Where the Mary River charters fish
The Mary River system lets one trip fish both fresh and salt water. Above the Shady Camp barrage are permanent, lily-lined billabongs; below it the tidal river splits into Sampan and Tommycut creeks and runs into Chambers Bay.
• Shady Camp barrage — Where fresh water meets the tide — the best-known barra spot on the river, fished from boats and from the bank
• Shady Camp billabong — Freshwater side: barramundi, saratoga and tarpon along the lilies, edges and rock bars
• Sampan and Tommycut creek mouths — Big run-off barra trolled where floodwater pours out into Chambers Bay
• Chambers Bay reefs and shoals — Reached from Shady Camp on neap tides: black jewfish, golden snapper, blue salmon and cod
• Corroboree Billabong and Hardies Lagoon — A large billabong system closer to Darwin with about 45 km of fishable water: barra, saratoga and tarpon in the dry
• Rockhole — Boat ramp in Mary River National Park giving access to the river channels
When to go
The Mary is fished most of the year, but the seasons change where and how you fish.
• Run-off (late February to May) — Peak time for big barra as the floodplains drain — below the barrage and at the creek mouths, best around March–April
• Dry season (about May to September) — Consistent billabong fishing for barra, saratoga and tarpon; generally smaller fish
• Build-up (September to mid-December) — Big barra move back to the river mouths and come back on the bite, especially on neap tides
• Wet season — Big fish are possible between storms, but Shady Camp access is 4WD only
• Threadfin salmon — Arrive later in the run-off and through the build-up
Getting there and trip styles
The Mary River is east of Darwin via the Stuart and Arnhem Highways. Corroboree Billabong is about 108 km from Darwin, and Shady Camp is about 200 km — around 1 hour 40 minutes by car, or about two hours towing a boat. Point Stuart Wilderness Lodge, 20 minutes from Shady Camp, is reachable on sealed road almost all the way.
Most visitors fish with a Darwin-based guide on a day trip — pick-up from your Darwin accommodation is usually about 5am, fishing Corroboree or Shady Camp and returning mid to late afternoon. In the run-off, many guides run multi-day trips based at a lodge near Shady Camp.
Rules and crocodile safety
The Mary River has its own fish management zone with a lower barramundi possession limit than the rest of the Territory and gear restrictions, including tighter rules close to the Shady Camp barrage. Check the current NT Fisheries rules before you go — your guide will also brief you. Recreational fishing below the barrage is open year-round.
You'll need an NT Parks Visitor Pass to enter Mary River National Park (NT residents are exempt), and campsites at Shady Camp must be booked online. The Mary has one of the highest densities of saltwater crocodiles in the world: don't swim, stay well back from the water's edge, and never wade in to free a snagged lure.

Frequently asked questions
How much does a fishing charter on the Mary River cost?
Across the 11 charters listed on the Mary River on Hooked Trips, trips start from $225 per person. Prices depend on trip length, boat size and season, so check each operator's trips for exact pricing.
What fish can you catch on the Mary River?
Charters on the Mary River most often target Barramundi, Black Jewfish, Golden Snapper, Queenfish, Threadfin Salmon and Mud Crab. Each listing shows the species that operator targets and when they're in season.
When is the best time to go fishing on the Mary River?
The run-off from late February to May — peaking in March and April — is the time for big barramundi below the Shady Camp barrage and at the creek mouths. The build-up (September to mid-December) is the other big-fish window, while the dry season gives consistent billabong fishing for barra, saratoga and tarpon.
Do I need a fishing licence in the Northern Territory?
No. The NT doesn't require a recreational fishing licence. Possession and size limits apply (including for barramundi), and some areas — such as parts of Arnhem Land and other Aboriginal land and waters — need access permits, so check with your operator.
How far is the Mary River from Darwin?
Corroboree Billabong is about 108 km east of Darwin, and Shady Camp is about 200 km — roughly 1 hour 40 minutes by car via the Stuart and Arnhem Highways. Most guided day trips pick up from Darwin accommodation at around 5am.
Can I do a Mary River fishing trip as a day trip from Darwin?
Yes. Several Darwin-based guides run day trips to Corroboree Billabong or Shady Camp with accommodation pick-up, returning mid to late afternoon. Run-off trips are often multi-day and based at a lodge near Shady Camp.
Can you fish from the bank at Shady Camp?
Yes — the barrage is a well-known land-based spot. Special gear rules apply close to the barrage wall, and crocodile numbers are very high, so keep well back from the edge.
Do I need a park pass for the Mary River?
Visitors need an NT Parks Visitor Pass for Mary River National Park; NT residents are exempt. Campsites, including at Shady Camp, must be booked online before you arrive.
Is there a closed season on the Mary River?
Not for recreational fishing below the Shady Camp barrage, which has been open year-round since 2014. The Mary River fish management zone does have its own possession limits and gear rules.
