Goulburn Valley Fly Fishing / Our home water
Guided trout fishing / Alexandra, Victoria
The Goulburn
by drift boat
On a recent spring drift towards Alexandra, one of our guides and a client spent about an hour on one rising brown. It took several changes of tippet and fly before it ate. They had kilometres of river ahead of them, but that one fish was worth stopping for.
That’s what I like about fishing the Goulburn from a drift boat. We can reach a long bank that’s difficult to fish on foot, then pull in below the willows and give a rising trout a proper go. How much river we cover and how long we take to do it, is up to the fishing.

Fishing with a guide on the oars
Along the willows
There’s a fishing position at either end of the boat. With a mate up front or behind you and your guide in the middle, you can both fish as the bank goes by. A short cast from the boat can keep your fly beside the willows or in a bubble line for way longer than a big cast across the current.
The river we fish one day isn’t always the one we find the next. A change in the releases from Lake Eildon can put water through the trees or uncover a gravel bar. We choose the stretch, the start time and the flies around what the river is doing and how you’d like to fish.

Choose your drift
Five hours or eight?
Half day / A$850 per boat
Fish a particular window
In summer we might put in early, before the heat builds, or wait for the evening rise. In spring and autumn the better fishing can be in the middle of the day. Five hours gives us time to fish a section properly without needing the whole day.
A good choice for a first drift or when you need to be back at a particular time.
Full day / A$990 per boat
Give the river a day
Eight hours opens up longer stretches, including water downstream of Alexandra. Bring your lunch. We can spend the morning working the banks, stop on the fish that demand a little patience, and still have the afternoon to see what the next bend holds.
Our usual recommendation when the Goulburn is the reason for your trip.
Bringing a mate? You can book alone and add a second angler later at no extra guiding charge. Just tell us beforehand so we have the right gear aboard. We never pair separate bookings.

Reading the Goulburn
Different water through the season
Spring
A little warmth and cloud can bring mayflies off through the day. Caddis are always around and termites appear on the humid afternoons. A rise in a bubble line often requires some recon to get the fly and drift just right.
Summer
When the river is up, the edges come into their own. Undercut banks. Backwaters. A hopper or cicada fished close to the grass is often a better prospect than the fast flows of the main current.
Autumn
As flows ease, the flows from the dam ease and the fishing changes. On a mild afternoon there can be times when the mayflies hatch and we must slow down and be more precise. Other years, the terrestrials keep going until mid-May.
These are patterns we look for, not a promise of a hatch. Some days we fish a dry fly the whole way; on others a nymph or a streamer is the better choice.
Your day on the water
First drift or familiar water?
You don’t need to be a distance caster to enjoy the boat. A newer angler can have some instruction on the go, as well help deciding where to put the fly and what to do with the line once it lands. Someone who fishes regularly might prefer a quiet word from the oars and the chance to work a fish out for themselves.
Tell us about both anglers when booking, especially when one has never held a fly rod. We’ll talk honestly about whether to start in the boat or spend a session learning the cast on land first.

Thornton / 1994
We started with rafts
David Pickering and Geoff Hall introduced guided floating to the Goulburn in 1994. For the first sixteen years we guided from rafts. Then we began bringing in the purpose-built fibreglass drift boats that are at the centre of our guiding today.
Our team has made more than 10,000 guided drifts on this river. Where that history counts for a guest is usually a small decision: which bank to work in a westerly, which stretch stays clearer after rain, or when to stop rowing and wait on a pool.

Friends, families & clubs
A day together, or a few days away
More than two anglers?
Four friends can fish the same stretch in two boats, with a guide in each. One boat can work the bank while the other fishes farther out, then come together for lunch. We have six purpose-built boats in the team; how many we can run on your date depends on guide availability and river conditions.

The Goulburn River Journey
For a group travelling here to fish, the Journey links several days on the Goulburn, from below Eildon Pondage towards Molesworth. The standard trip is four fishing days, with a three-day option. We plan the boats, sections and accommodation with your group.

Before you come
What to bring
We supply
Your guide, the drift boat, lifejackets, flies and drinking water. Rods and reels are available when needed. Bring a favourite outfit or ask us to have one ready for you.
You bring
Lunch and snacks, a drink bottle, a hat, polarised sunglasses, sunscreen and a layer for a change of weather. Bring a current Victorian recreational fishing licence unless exempt.
