Private tuition / Alexandra & Thornton, Victoria
What would you like to work on?
You might be picking up a fly rod for the first time. Or you might know exactly where a trout is feeding, but your fly skates past it as soon as the current catches your line. We can start with how to hold the rod and cast, or spend a morning on that one troublesome drift.
Come on your own for a one-to-one lesson, or share it with a friend or partner. Your instructor works with just the people in your booking.

Half day or full day?
A half day to find your feet
A good first lesson, or a chance to sort out one thing that’s been giving you trouble. We can start with a short cast on grass, then take it to the water. Already fishing? Bring the cast that keeps letting you down and we can spend the session on that instead.
A full day to put it together
Choose the full day when you’d like more time fishing with your instructor. You can practise a cast, use it on a run, then try it again where a faster current runs between you and the fly. There’s time to watch what the fly does and make the next cast with that in mind.
From practice to fishing
A cast you can use on the river
We’ve been teaching on the Goulburn for more than 30 years. One thing we come back to is giving every cast a purpose. A roll cast makes more sense when there’s a tree behind you. We’ll show you the cast, let you try it, then help you change one thing at a time. The aim is a fly that lands where you meant it to, not a long cast for its own sake.
Beside the water, we might turn over a stone and compare the insects underneath with the flies in the box. Then we’ll look for a place a trout could hold and give that fly a drift. On moving water, watching the line can tell you why a well-placed fly suddenly starts to drag.
For a first lesson we can cover setting up the rod, wading safely and what to do when a trout takes, from lifting the rod to landing the fish. For a returning angler we might spend that same time on accuracy, dry-fly presentation or fishing a nymph below the surface. We finish with something specific to practise next time.
Before you come
No need to buy a rod. We supply rods, reels, lines, leaders and flies, with waders and boots available when needed. You’re welcome to bring your own outfit, especially if that’s the one you’ll fish with afterwards.
Where we meet. We teach around Alexandra and Thornton by arrangement throughout the year. We’ll confirm the meeting point and start time with you. Whether we use a lake or a stretch of river depends on the season, the conditions and what you’d like to learn.
What to bring. Pack a hat, polarised sunglasses, a drink bottle and a layer for the weather. Bring lunch for a full day. For practical fishing, bring a Victorian recreational fishing licence unless you’re exempt.
Would another session suit you better?
Prefer learning with a group? Try the two-day beginner workshop . Already cast comfortably but unsure where to fish? Look at private streamcraft tuition . For a day spent mainly fishing rather than practising, choose guided bank and wade fishing .
Arrange your private lesson
Choose a date online or tell me when you’re coming, whether there’ll be one or two of you and what you’d most like help with. A text is often easiest when I’m on the river.
