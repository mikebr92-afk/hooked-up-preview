Giant Black Marlin Fishing
The pinnacle of the sport of big game fishing!​
Every year between September and December, giant black marlin — predominantly large females, many weighing up to and exceeding 1,000lbs — gather along a stretch of reef edge known as the Ribbon Reefs, approximately 30 km offshore along the Great Barrier Reef .
Alongside these larger females are smaller male fish, commonly in the 200–400lb range, often present in greater numbers.
It’s this seasonal concentration of heavy tackle black marlin that has made this region one of the most consistent giant marlin fisheries anywhere in the world.
Giant black marlin are big fish in every sense of the word.
Powerful and unpredictable, they’re known for their explosive aerial displays — often launching clear of the water in a way that stops everyone on board in their tracks.
The adrenaline is immediate, and it’s not just the angler who feels it. It’s this combination of size, power and spectacle that keeps anglers, captains and crew returning season after season.
About the Ribbon Reefs & the reef experience
The Ribbon Reefs are a chain of narrow, breaking reef that run ribbon-like parallel to the coast of northern Queensland. The steep drop-offs and current lines create ideal black marlin feeding grounds, one reason why the fish return each year. Anglers travel from across Australia and overseas specifically to fish this short season window, when conditions align and the fish are present in numbers.
The setting itself is part of the experience. This section of the Great Barrier Reef is remote and largely untouched. And absolutely beautiful. 
By night, vessels anchor on the protected lee side of the reef, often just minutes from where they will fish the following morning. There’s no long commute — you’re already there!
Outside of targeting marlin, the reef offers additional opportunities. Mornings often involve catching fresh bait — frequently quality sportfish in their own right — as well as jigging for dogtooth tuna, coral trout and throwing poppers for giant trevally. During downtime, guests can swim and snorkel sheltered reef bays in clear, protected water over beautiful coral gardens.
It’s a working fishery, but it operates within one of the most remarkable natural environments in the world.
