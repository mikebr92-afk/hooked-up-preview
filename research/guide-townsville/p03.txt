General FAQs 
Fishing Styles 
What’s the difference between deep sea fishing, reef fishing, and game fishing?
• Deep Sea Fishing : Targets fish in deeper offshore waters (typically over 100m deep). This often involves bottom fishing for large reef species or trolling for pelagic fish.
• Reef Fishing : Focuses on fishing around coral reefs, targeting species like Coral Trout, Red Emperor, and Nannygai. This style of fishing is usually done in shallower waters (30m–80m deep).
• Game Fishing : A more intense style of fishing that involves targeting large, powerful fish like Marlin, Tuna, and Sailfish. This is typically done through trolling (dragging lures or bait behind the boat).
What species of fish can I expect to catch?
The species you’ll encounter depend on the type of fishing and the time of year:
• Reef Fishing : Coral Trout, Red Emperor, Nannygai, Sweetlip, Spanish Mackerel
• Deep Sea Fishing : Jobfish, Snapper, Goldband Snapper, Trevally
• Game Fishing : Marlin (Black & Blue), Sailfish, Tuna, Mahi Mahi (Dorado), Wahoo
How do I decide which type of trip is best for me?
You don’t have to choose! Our trips are flexible, and we adjust the fishing style based on the conditions, what’s biting, and guest preferences. On any given trip, you might be bottom fishing on the reef, chasing deep-sea species, or trolling for big pelagics —or a mix of all three. Whether you’re after quality table fish, a deep-water challenge, or an adrenaline-pumping fight, we’ll make sure you get the best experience on the day.
Fishing Methods 
What fishing techniques do you use on your trips?
Our trips are flexible, and we use a variety of fishing techniques depending on conditions, target species, and guest preferences. These include:
• Bottom Fishing – Dropping baited hooks to the seabed to target species like Red Emperor, Nannygai, and Coral Trout .
• Trolling – Dragging lures or bait behind the boat to hook Spanish Mackerel, Tuna, Marlin, and Wahoo .
• Jigging – Using heavy metal lures (jigs) that are dropped and worked up through the water column to attract Dogtooth Tuna, Trevally, and Jobfish .
• Popping – Casting large surface lures (poppers) to create a commotion and trigger strikes from aggressive species like Giant Trevally (GTs) .
• Live Baiting – Using fresh live bait to target big predators like Cobia, Marlin, and Barracuda .
What kind of lures and bait do you use?
We supply a variety of high-quality bait and lures, depending on the fishing method:
• Trolling – Hard-body diving lures, skirted lures, and rigged dead bait for pelagic species.
• Jigging – Butterfly jigs, knife jigs, and slow-pitch jigs to work the mid to deep-water zones.
• Popping – Large stickbaits and poppers to entice explosive surface strikes.
• Bottom Fishing – Fresh squid, pilchards, and cut bait for reef species.
• Live Baiting – Scad, yakkas, and fusiliers to attract big game fish.
Can I bring my own fishing gear?
Absolutely! If you have a favourite rod, reel, or lure setup, feel free to bring it along. If not, we provide high-quality fishing gear for hire.
Which method is best for catching big fish?
It depends on the species you’re targeting:
• Want a Marlin or Sailfish? → Trolling & Live Baiting
• Chasing Giant Trevally? → Popping & Jigging
• Looking for a tasty Red Emperor? → Bottom Fishing
• After a mixed bag of reef & pelagic species? → A combination of all methods!
Do I need experience with these techniques?
Not at all! Our crew is happy to guide you, whether you’re a first-timer or an experienced angler looking to refine your skills.
Day Trips vs. Liveaboard Adventures 
What’s the difference between a day trip and a liveaboard trip?
• Day Trips: Shorter trips (usually 12–24 hours) that focus on specific fishing styles. You’ll return to port at the end of the trip.
• Liveaboard Trips: Multi-day fishing expeditions where you stay on the boat, allowing for extended fishing time and access to more remote locations.
What are the advantages of a liveaboard trip?
• More time to fish, with early morning and late-night opportunities
• Access to untouched fishing grounds that aren’t reachable on day trips
• A fully catered experience with meals, comfortable sleeping arrangements, and plenty of time to relax between fishing sessions
Which trip is better for beginners?
Both can be suitable, but day trips are ideal if you’re new to offshore fishing and want a shorter experience. Liveaboards are great if you’re comfortable on the water and keen for a full fishing adventure.
Do I need to bring food or drinks?
Nope! All our trips include freshly prepared meals, snacks, and non-alcoholic drinks. BYO alcohol in moderation.
Are liveaboard trips only for hardcore fishermen?
Not at all! While these trips are perfect for dedicated anglers, they’re also great for those who want a mix of fishing, relaxation, and exploring the reef.

Fishing Quest FAQs 
What happens if the weather is bad?
Safety is our top priority. If the weather is unsafe for departure, we’ll reschedule your trip or offer a credit toward a future booking. We recommend travel insurance to cover any non-refundable costs.
When should I arrive at the boat?
We ask that you arrive half an hour prior to departure for the Welcome Tour and Safety Briefing.
What do I need to bring?
We provide all bait, tackle, and meals, and alcohol (available for purchase) but you should bring:
• Sunscreen, hat, and sunglasses
• Enclosed footwear
• Light clothing and a warm layer for cooler evenings
• Any personal medications
Can I bring my own fishing gear?
Absolutely! You’re welcome to bring your own rods, reels, and tackle. We also have high-quality fishing gear available for hire.
What happens to the fish I catch?
For day trips, you can take your cleaned fish home in your own esky. On extended trips (72+ hours), we fillet, bag, and freeze your catch for you. We also offer airline-approved Styrofoam boxes for transport.
What is the minimum age for kids?
For offshore fishing trips, children must weigh at least 32kg (typically around 10 years old) and must be accompanied by a parent or guardian.
What if I get seasick?
If you’re prone to seasickness, we recommend taking medication before departure. Once on board, fresh air, staying hydrated, and keeping your eyes on the horizon can help.
What if I need to cancel my trip?
Deposits are non-refundable, but you may transfer your spot to someone else or rebook for a future trip. Cancellation policies vary based on notice given—see our Terms & Conditions for details.
