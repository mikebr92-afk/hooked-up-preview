Gippsland Lakes Fishing Charter — What to Expect on Your First Trip
Chris Jordan 
Jun 25 
2 min read 
Booking your first guided fishing charter on the Gippsland Lakes can feel both exciting and slightly daunting — especially if you're new to lure fishing or estuary fishing altogether. Having guided many first-timers on this system, I can tell you the most common feeling at the end of the day is wondering why it took so long to book. 
The System — Bigger Than You Think 
The Gippsland Lakes is Australia's largest coastal lake system, covering interconnected waterways including Lake King, Lake Victoria, Lake Wellington and the Mitchell, Nicholson and Tambo Rivers. The scale is part of what makes it both impressive and challenging. On every charter I run here, my first job is narrowing down the productive areas based on season, weather, tides and what the fish are doing on that particular day. Without that local knowledge, it’s easy to spend a full day covering water that holds very little fish. 
What We Fish For 
Most of my Gippsland Lakes charters focus on black bream, yellowfin bream and dusky flathead using lure-fishing techniques exclusively — no bait. Depending on conditions, we might be fishing shallow flats for topwater bream, working crankbaits along timber-lined river banks, or running soft plastics across deeper channel edges. Every session is different, and I adapt throughout the day to stay on active fish. 
What First-Timers Often Discover 
Beginners are often surprised by how technical estuary lure fishing can be. Small details like casting angle, retrieve speed and boat positioning make a genuine difference to results. Part of what I enjoy most about guiding first-timers is watching that click happen — when the fundamentals start making sense and fish begin responding. A guided trip fast-tracks that learning process considerably. 
What to Bring 
I supply all rods, reels, lures and safety equipment — you don't need to bring any fishing gear. What you do need is weather-appropriate clothing, a good pair of polarised sunglasses (they make a significant difference when sight-fishing), and food and drinks for the session. I keep things relaxed and practical on the water. 
Flexibility Is Everything 
One of the biggest advantages of a guided charter is adaptability. If one location or technique slows down, I relocate or change approach to keep clients on fish. On a system as large as the Gippsland Lakes, that flexibility — knowing where to go next — is something that takes years to develop independently. 
Who Is a Gippsland Lakes Charter For? 
In short — everyone. I've guided complete beginners who had never held a fishing rod, experienced anglers chasing a personal-best bream, families wanting a memorable day out and mates who simply want to fish seriously for a full day. The Gippsland Lakes accommodates all of it. For many visitors to East Gippsland, a guided fishing trip becomes one of the highlights of the entire trip — the combination of stunning scenery, genuine trophy fish opportunities and local knowledge is hard to replicate.
