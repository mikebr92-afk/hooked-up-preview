Dusky Flathead on Soft PlasticsWhat Works in Gippsland's Estuaries —
Chris Jordan 
Jun 25 
2 min read 
Soft plastics have transformed dusky flathead fishing across East Gippsland's estuary systems. From the Gippsland Lakes to Mallacoota Inlet and Bemm River, they consistently produce fish across all seasons — and they're one of the first techniques I introduce on every guided charter. 
Keep Lure Selection Simple 
One of the biggest mistakes I see beginners make on my charters is overcomplicating lure selection. In most Gippsland estuaries, flathead primarily feed on small baitfish and prawns — meaning paddle-tail plastics between 70mm and 120mm cover most situations effectively. I carry a small selection of proven colours and sizes rather than an overwhelming tackle bag, and I teach clients to do the same. 
Colour Selection 
Natural colours like olive, motor oil, bloodworm and pearl tend to work exceptionally well in the clear water conditions common across Gippsland's systems. In dirty or tannin-stained water after rain, brighter colours such as chartreuse or pink can be highly effective. I generally start with naturals and only switch if I'm not getting interest after covering a productive area thoroughly. 
Retrieve Technique 
Retrieve speed is equally important — and this is where I see most beginners go wrong. Flathead are ambush predators that sit on sandy patches, weed edges and drop-offs waiting for prey to pass overhead. A simple slow-hop retrieve along the bottom is often all that's needed. I emphasise long pauses on my charters — letting the lure sink back toward the fish often triggers strikes that a continuous retrieve would never produce. 
Jighead Weight 
Matching jighead weight to conditions makes a significant difference. On the shallow sand flats of Lake Tyers or the upper Mallacoota system, lighter jigheads allow plastics to stay in the strike zone longer and create a more natural presentation. Heavier jigheads become important when fishing the deeper channels of the Gippsland Lakes system where maintaining bottom contact is the priority. 
Where to Fish 
Some of East Gippsland's most productive flathead areas include shallow sand banks, channel edges adjacent to weed beds and areas where baitfish are visibly concentrated. Wind-blown shorelines can also fish exceptionally well during warmer months as baitfish are pushed into the shallows. On my charters I focus on covering water efficiently — flathead are a searching species and persistence pays off. 
What Guided Clients Often Discover 
Guided clients are regularly surprised by how effective a simple technique can be. The key is maintaining bottom contact, fishing likely ambush zones thoroughly and resisting the urge to move on too quickly. For many anglers who join me, there's no more addictive estuary experience than watching a big dusky flathead inhale a soft plastic in shallow water. Once that happens, most people are hooked for life.
