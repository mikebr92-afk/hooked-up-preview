Trout: what to know
Tasmania is one of the world's great wild brown trout destinations, and Victoria's High Country adds river and lake fly fishing within a few hours of Melbourne. Professional guides take anglers sight-fishing on the lakes of Tasmania's Central Highlands, wading the meadow streams around Launceston and the Northern Midlands, and drifting the Derwent near Hobart.
Most guides supply rods, waders and flies and include tuition, so trips suit first-timers as well as experienced fly anglers. In Tasmania, anyone 14 or older needs their own Inland Fisheries Service angling licence, even on a guided trip.

Frequently asked questions
How much does a trout fishing guide cost?
Across the 7 trout fishing guides listed on Hooked Trips, shared trips start from $500 per person and private charters from $450 per group. Prices depend on trip length, boat size and location, so check each operator's trips for exact pricing.
Where can I book a trout fishing guide?
Hooked Trips lists trout fishing guides in Launceston & the North (3), Central Highlands (2), Lake Eildon & the High Country (1) and Hobart & Storm Bay (1).
When is the best time for trout?
Most Tasmanian trout waters are open from the first Saturday in August to the Sunday nearest 30 April (1 August 2026 to 2 May 2027). Designated rainbow trout waters open in October, and some lakes, including most of Great Lake, are open all year. Guides report dry fly fishing improving through spring and summer.
Do I need a fishing licence on a charter?
It depends on the state. In NSW, anglers on a licensed charter boat are covered by the operator's licence. Queensland and the Northern Territory don't require a recreational fishing licence for saltwater fishing. Size and bag limits apply everywhere.
