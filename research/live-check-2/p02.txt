Fishing in Tasmania
Tasmania combines cold-water ocean fishing with some of the world's best wild trout fishing. Charters work Storm Bay, the Tasman Peninsula and the East Coast, while professional guides take anglers to the lakes and rivers of the Central Highlands.
Saltwater trips target southern bluefin tuna, striped trumpeter and flathead; inland guides specialise in sight-fishing for brown and rainbow trout.
Fishing regions in Tasmania
Tasmania's charter and guide industry splits into salt water in the south-east and east, and trout country inland. Boats work out of Hobart and the Tasman Peninsula, with East Coast charters at St Helens, while professional trout guides operate from Hobart, Miena in the Central Highlands, Launceston and Cressy.
• Hobart & Storm Bay — Inshore bottom fishing for flathead, salmon and squid, offshore tuna and striped trumpeter, and Derwent estuary fly fishing
• Tasman Peninsula — Pirates Bay and Port Arthur for southern bluefin and albacore tuna, deep reef fishing and calmer bay trips
• St Helens & the East Coast — Georges Bay and offshore reefs and game fishing; charters run both inside the bay and offshore
• Central Highlands — Great Lake, Arthurs Lake, the shallow lagoons and the Western Lakes for wild brown trout on fly
• Launceston & the North — Midlands rivers such as Brumbys Creek, the South Esk and Meander for river fly fishing
Seasons across the state
Salt water fishes all year, with tuna off the Tasman Peninsula from about March to August. Striped trumpeter are closed from 1 September to 31 October. Most trout waters open on 1 August 2026 and close on 2 May 2027; Great Lake and several lowland waters stay open all year.
Licences
Rod-and-line fishing in marine waters needs no licence; rock lobster, abalone, scallops, nets and set lines do, and charter clients must hold any licence the activity requires. Inland, everyone 14 or older needs an IFS angling licence, guided clients included; short-term 48-hour, 7-day and 28-day licences are available and junior licences (14-17) are free.
Frequently asked questions
How much does a fishing charter in Tasmania cost?
Across the 9 charters listed in Tasmania on Hooked Trips, shared trips start from $198 per person and private charters from $750 per group. Prices depend on trip length, boat size and season, so check each operator's trips for exact pricing.
What fish can you catch in Tasmania?
Charters in Tasmania most often target Brown Trout, Rainbow Trout, Flathead, Australian Salmon, Southern Bluefin Tuna and Squid. Each listing shows the species that operator targets and when they're in season.
When is the best time to go fishing in Tasmania?
Southern bluefin tuna are an autumn and early-winter target off the Tasman Peninsula and East Coast, while the trout season in most lakes and rivers runs from spring to autumn. Check each operator's trips for exact timing.
Do I need a fishing licence in Tasmania?
Not for rod-and-line fishing in the sea: Tasmania only requires sea licences for things like rock lobster, abalone, scallops, nets and set lines. Inland waters are different. Anyone 14 or older fishing for trout needs their own Inland Fisheries Service angling licence, including on a guided trip; 48-hour, 7-day, 28-day and season licences are available.
Do I need a fishing licence on a Tasmanian charter boat?
Not for rod and line in the sea. NRE Tas says charter clients need the appropriate recreational licence only for licensed activities such as rock lobster, abalone or scallops. Trout fishing in inland waters always needs an Inland Fisheries Service angling licence for anyone 14 or older.
When does the Tasmanian trout season open?
Most waters open on the first Saturday in August (1 August 2026) and close on the Sunday nearest 30 April (2 May 2027). Designated rainbow trout waters run from 3 October 2026 to 30 May 2027, and some waters, including Great Lake, are open all year.
