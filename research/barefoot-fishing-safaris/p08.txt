With the Top End's specialist fly fishing guide
Tropical fly fishing is different
Fly fishing up north is different from any trout stream or lake you've set foot on. It calls for a few different skills, different setups — and very different flies.
Life revolves around the moon and tides up here. It's the number one factor in your chances of success each day you fish.
"Up here the fish are big and angry — and we don't wear tweed." — Watty 
Very different flies — tied for big, angry tropical fish. 
When to come
Three fly seasons in one Top End
You can fly fish all year round in the Top End, but the best of it runs from late February to the end of November — and what you'll be casting at changes with the water.
Run-off · Late Feb – Early June
The big rivers of Kakadu, fishing the colour changes as the floodwaters drain — blind casting, not sight casting, for big barra and king threadfin.
Dry season · Early Jun – End Aug
Once the run-off trips finish, the sight casting starts: Bynoe's clean shallow water at its best, plus the freshwater billabongs.
Build-up · Early Sep – End Nov
Sight casting and the freshwater billabongs again, in warmer conditions — the water's alive before the wet arrives.
Darwin Harbour
World-class fly fishing on Darwin's doorstep
Darwin Harbour has everything a tropical fly fisher looks for. When you get the tides right and know where to look, it's all there waiting for you — minutes from town.
Harbour silver — a barramundi that ate the fly. 
Darwin Harbour / A Barefoot speciality
Milkfish on fly — and nobody else is guiding for them
Milkfish are one of the great prizes in saltwater fly fishing: fast, spooky, and famous for running a long way into the backing. Most anglers travel to Christmas Island or the Seychelles to find them.
There is a population right here in Darwin Harbour, minutes from town — and Barefoot is the only operation in Darwin guiding for them on fly.
They are not easy. Getting one to eat is an achievement on its own, and holding on afterwards is another thing again. If milkfish are on your list, say so when you enquire and Watty will build the tides around them.
A Darwin Harbour milkfish, fly still pinned in the corner of its jaw. 
Bynoe Harbour
Sight-casting barra — and whatever else you're dreaming of
Saltwater fly fishing for barramundi in Bynoe Harbour is the headline act — hard-fighting fish, accurate casts in challenging tidal conditions, and insider techniques to land more fish on the fly.
Depending on your goals, Watty creates a personalised fishing plan to suit your desires, your abilities and — of course — the tides. Sight-cast at cruising barra all trip, chase big pelagics all week, slide gurglers over the lily pads for saratoga, or do a bit of everything.
Guests fish a high-quality catch-and-release adventure in pristine wilderness. The main species: barramundi, mangrove jack, threadfin salmon, saratoga, golden snapper, queenfish, giant trevally and Spanish mackerel — plus about forty other tropical estuary and reef fish.
Bynoe chrome, taken on fly. 
Freshwater
Top End saratoga on fly
Saratoga are without doubt one of the best fish to target with fly tackle. They love crunching surface flies first thing in the morning, and they'll eat a subsurface presentation as the day goes on.
Billabong gold — a saratoga from the pandanus country.

Your guide, every single day
No blow-in guides — you fish with me, every single day, two anglers to the boat. I'm an IGFA captain & guide, a PFIGA-certified fishing instructor, and the Top End's specialist fly fishing guide.
Whether you're new to the salt or you've chased flats fish around the world, the plan gets built around you — your casting, your goals, and the tides.

How a safari runs
Your fly fishing itinerary
Trips of 3–7 days. Depending on the length of your trip you can fish up to four different areas — Darwin Harbour, Corroboree Billabong, Dundee Beach and Bynoe Harbour.
Arrival day
Your ute is waiting at Darwin airport when you land. Drive out the night before and wake up on the water, or head out on the morning of day one and meet Watty at the ramp — whichever suits your flights.
Fishing days
Days begin as the sun comes up, about 6:30am — breakfast at your lodgings or on the boat. Then it's tides, flats, billabongs or bluewater as the plan dictates.
Knock-off
You're finished fishing and off the water around 4pm each day — unless you nominate to pull up earlier.
Built around you
A detailed itinerary is confirmed with you through the booking process, so the trip suits your needs, your casting and your goals.
