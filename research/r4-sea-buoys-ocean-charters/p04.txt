About
A working fishing boat, an Islander skipper, and a place he has never wanted to leave.
Scott fishing with his Dad, Larry Wilson 
Scott
Fishing at the jetty is Scott’s first memory of island life.
Once he was old enough he had a run of tinnies, which is how most Islander kids learn, and how you learn this coast properly: the reef edges, where the water moves, which corners you do not go around in a southerly. He went on to run vessels for other island businesses, guiding numerous visitors through the Lord Howe Island Marine Park.
Scott left the island in his twenties and spent years working in guiding and hospitality. Southern Ocean Lodge on Kangaroo Island, Bedarra Island off the Queensland coast, and commercial fishing vessels out of northern Queensland.
Then he came home to fish.
Tukula came first, and after her Whispen. Working boats, both of them, fishing commercially and supplying the island’s restaurants with some of the best fish in the world.
Now Fin. She is the first boat Scott has owned that is in survey to carry passengers, after years of visitors asking to come out fishing with him.
Fin
Fin is a White Pointer 9.40 Pro Sportsman, built in New Zealand in 2014, aluminium, with 8mm plate through the hull. She was custom built for Scott’s uncle, and Scott worked her alongside him for years before buying her from him.
Fin was specified and optimised for Lord Howe’s needs, rather than a brochure. Shaft drive, because reliability matters when you are 600km from anywhere. A large marlin door on the starboard side, which meant the hull had to be re-engineered around it. A removable 1,200 litre deck tank, and a heavy-duty off-road trailer so she can come out of the water in cyclone season. A lot of how she is laid out came from how fishing is done here.
When her build was completed, she was driven home. Eight hundred and thirty nautical miles across the Tasman from the Bay of Islands.
She was built to survey standards and remains in survey today. She is rated to carry ten passengers and we take six at most, because small groups are the point. Scott knows every part of her, because he was aboard for most of what she has done.
Taking people out
People have asked Scott to take them fishing for years. He has said yes to a few of them, and never for money.
Mostly the chefs. On an island where every restaurant runs flat out through the season, the people cooking your dinner work through the daylight and rarely get out on the water. A day off and a spare seat has fixed that more than once.
Some of the best friendships Scott has are with people he met at sea, in weather, on a boat. That is the part of this he wanted to keep when the business started.
Chelsea
Chelsea Holden runs everything ashore. Bookings, enquiries, safety systems, permits and reporting. If you have emailed or messaged us, you have spoken to Chelsea.
She is also a photographer, which you’ll notice in the array of photos throughout the site. For any enquiries for professional photography while you are on the island, please contact her on www.chelseascottphoto.com.au 
Why now
Scott and Chelsea have two kids, and they watch everything.
The intention is to show them how this works. Not just how to catch a fish, but how you look after the water you take it from, how you make a living from a place without wearing it out, and why the rules exist here. If they want it later, it will be there.
How we run
Sea Buoys Ocean Charters is a small operation. One boat, one skipper, a deckhand when the trip calls for one, and usually one trip a day. That is deliberate. It means the boat gets looked after, the days do not get rushed, and nobody is tired.
Scott holds a Coxswain Grade 1 Near Coastal certificate. Fin holds a current Certificate of Survey and an AMSA Certificate of Operation, and we run to a documented safety management system. We are permitted to operate commercially within the Lord Howe Island Marine Park under permit MEAA26/164 and are licensed by the Lord Howe Island Board to have a commercial operation on Lord Howe Island.
Weather led is not a figure of speech. We do not depart on fishing trips in more than 20 knots, or in swell over 3 metres, and Fin cannot safely leave the lagoon on a tide below 0.3 metres — which is why departure times move through the week. Those are our own limits, and they sit alongside whatever the survey, the Certificate of Operation and the marine park permit require, whichever is tighter.
Lord Howe keeps its visitor numbers low on purpose, and the marine park exists for good reason. We work within both. We fish to limits, follow the rules, and report our activity to the marine park as a condition of operating here.
