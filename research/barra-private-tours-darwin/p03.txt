Activity details 
Want to know the tidal conditions for barra fishing or reef fishing in Darwin Harbour? We’ve got you covered. Our tailored schedule is carefully measured around the best tidal windows, giving you the highest chance of success on the water. You’ll be able to check departure times and see exactly what type of fishing experience is planned.
Whether it’s chasing trophy barramundi or heading coastal line for reef action in the Darwin Harbour . 
Guided by our friendly and highly experienced fishing guide, you’ll use premium light tackle and a variety of lures or baits suited to inshore trips. Any fish you decide to keep will be professionally cleaned and filleted, ready for you to take home and enjoy.
Shared Charter – Minimum & Maximum Numbers Minimum: 3 passengers | Maximum: 4 passengers 
Our trips are designed to run with at least 3 guests . If the minimum number is not met, the trip may need to be cancelled or rescheduled .
However, if you still wish to proceed, we can run the charter with fewer guests for a small additional fee per person to meet the minimum launch cost.
To cover fuel and labour costs, a minimum booking value of $800 is required for all shared charters.
Important: Final guest numbers will be confirmed 2 days prior to your trip.
This option allows you to lock in your date with the understanding that the charter may operate as either a shared or private experience , depending on final passenger numbers.

FAQ 
• 
Q 1: How do I choose between reef fishing and barra fishing? 
Reef Fishing 
If you’re looking for fast-paced action and the thrill of catching a variety of fish in a single trip, reef fishing is your best bet. You’ll enjoy excitement as you reel in species like coral trout, red emperor, and snapper.
Barra Fishing 
If you want to test your casting skills and target trophy-sized barramundi, barra fishing is the way to go. This adventure challenges anglers to land one of Australia’s most iconic sportfish in its natural habitat.
Q 2:Why 6 Hours Beats 4 
Darwin’s tides shift dramatically — often 4–5m in just a few hours — so timing is everything. A 4-hour trip usually only catches one phase of the tide cycle, while a 6-hour trip lets you fish across both the run-in and run-out, hitting more active feeding windows. It also gives access to more fishing grounds as water levels change, more time on the water relative to fixed travel time, and room to adapt if conditions or the bite shift. Simply put: more tide, more ground, more fish, less rush.
Contact us to check the tidal condition, and we’ll help you pick the perfect experience!
