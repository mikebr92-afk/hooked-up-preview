Pure adrenaline What is jig fishing?
Jigging is the fast, physical, heart-pumping end of fishing. You drop a weighted metal jig down deep, then work it back to the surface with sharp lifts and winds until a fish smashes it on the way up. The word jig means dance, and that is exactly what it is, a fast-paced dance between you and some of the hardest-fighting fish in Sydney’s water.
It is a genuine workout and an absolute rush, and it is deadly on kingfish, tuna and much more. Wahoo Fishing Charters supplies top-quality Shimano rods, metal jigs and the best live bait, and our champion crew coaches you through the technique, so no experience is needed to get amongst them.

What you'll catch What you'll jig up
Jigging tempts a huge range of Sydney's hardest-pulling fish, from blue-water pelagics to bruisers holding deep on the reefs. These are the species we chase most on a jigging charter.
Yellowtail Kingfish
The king of the jig. Brutal, dirty fighters that inhale a fast-worked jig over the reefs and wrecks.
Yellowfin Tuna
Jig one of these in the blue water and hang on, blistering runs and a serious workout.
Bonito
Fast, feisty and everywhere in the warmer months, bonito love a speed jig and are great fun.
Australian Salmon
School up in numbers and hammer a jig, non-stop action and a genuine light-tackle blast.
Snapper
Slow-pitch jigs worked over the deep reefs tempt big, quality snapper for the table.
Silver Trevally
Silver, strong and always keen, trevally school over the reefs and smash a jig with gusto.
How we fish Two styles of jigging
There is more than one way to work a jig, and we run whichever suits the fish and the ground on the day.
Mechanical (speed) jigging
Fast, rhythmic lifts and winds send a slim metal jig darting through the water column. It is the high-energy, arms-burning style that big kingfish and pelagics find impossible to resist. Be warned, it is completely addictive.
Slow-pitch jigging
A finesse style that flutters a jig enticingly over the deep reefs, tempting a huge range of species including snapper, trevally and more. It is less of a workout, deadly effective, and a great way to learn the ropes.
Your crew Learn from a champion crew
Jigging is all about technique, and there is no better way to learn than alongside a champion crew. George and the team have spent more than 30 years jigging Sydney’s water, and they know exactly which jig, which speed and which spot will get you connected.
They put the rod in your hands, set you over the fish and coach you through the lift-and-wind rhythm until it clicks, then talk you through the fight. When they say they will put you on the fish, 40 five-star Google reviews back them up.
